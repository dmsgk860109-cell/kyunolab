Editorial boundary

Careful language protects the archive.

Words like "some versions say," "the origin is uncertain," and "in later retellings" are not weak writing here. They are how the site keeps mystery from turning into false certainty.

Source And Story Rules

Public reading standard

Source Status

Kyunolab Mystery Archive separates verified fact from unverified legend. A documented place, book, custom, court record, newspaper item, or archived page can support context, but it does not automatically prove every strange claim attached to the story.

Source status labels show whether a page is discussing a public-domain source, documented tradition, modern retelling, speculative interpretation, source-limited claim, or original work.

Story Types

Some records are folklore explanations, some are urban legend readings, some are mystery notes, and some are original dialogue-free or prose-based retellings shaped by older motifs.

A modern retelling may preserve a familiar pattern without claiming to be the first version. A speculative interpretation may ask what a motif means without saying that the interpretation is proven.

Evidence And Limits

Articles should not use uncertain origins as certainty. If a page cannot verify a detail, it should name the limitation instead of hiding it.

Useful evidence can include dated publications, archive copies, folklore collections, maps, records, screenshots, or other material that can be checked independently.

How Readers Should Use This

When stronger evidence is missing, a page may still be valuable as a record of motif, circulation, setting, and reader memory. It should not turn that value into a false claim of proof.

Readers can enjoy atmosphere, folklore, and mystery while still noticing the difference between a documented fact, a repeated tradition, and a creative retelling.
