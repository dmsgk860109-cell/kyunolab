The source chain is narrow and chronological: a front-desk event at the Hotel del Coronado, a delayed exit signal in public memory, then a long interpretive afterlife.

This sequence shows how a documented hospitality object can shift from ledger utility to legend threshold.

Front Desk Entry as a Threshold Record

The incident begins at the registration desk of the Hotel del Coronado, a resort that had opened only a few years earlier. In November 1892, a young woman signed in under the name "Lottie A Bernard" and became one of the few visitors who would later define the hotel as much as its architecture did. The record of her entry did more than confirm a room assignment: it fixed a moment at the boundary where a traveler becomes an in-building participant, moving from public street to private guest history.

The chain is a single-event chain, not a city-wide myth cluster. One recorded arrival creates the first anchor for later retellings, and later accounts repeatedly return to that anchor because it is concrete and dateable. In this case, the guestbook entry is the practical object that marks who entered and when, long after memory has reworked the reasons.

Assumed Identity and Uncertain Initial Intent

Hotel staff later described this guest as a woman in distress and noted that she asked about someone who was expected but never arrived. Whether the woman was waiting, hiding, or simply alone that week, the register line itself preserved what could not be fully proven: that she had crossed the front threshold with a declared identity and contact details to match a hospitality flow.

The second sequence step is social, not just administrative. The same line that records her name becomes the interpretive starting point for many later claims, including the idea that a guestbook can act as a symbolic lockbox for unresolved intention. At this stage the event is still documentary: a person checked in, and then continued to report to the same desk through the stay.

The Stopping Point That Shaped the Story

Five days later, she was found dead on the exterior staircase leading toward the beach, and the register had no completed departure record to balance the arrival entry. That asymmetry is the decisive sequence break. What had been a routine front-desk line now looked like an unfinished transaction: entered, stayed, and never formally exited through the same administrative object.

That missing final signature is why the account gains threshold force. In practical hospitality terms, she remained in memory as a guest whose threshold action was incomplete. In folklore terms, that incompleteness is what let the story keep expanding rather than closing.

Coroner Review and Public Identification Delay

The 1892 coroner proceedings classified the death as a likely self-inflicted gunshot wound, while surviving period reporting described unresolved confusion about identity until later confirmation. The official and press trail moved from certainty to doubt and back to probable closure, but the register entry kept its own fixed role in that process.

This is where cause and action meet evidence limits. The administrative line did not create the death, but it does preserve the chain that connects the person at the desk, the body outside on the stairs, and the wider reporting that followed. That chain is the strongest factual spine in the legend.

Tour Routines and the Room as Memory Node

Once the event entered public memory, the hotel formalized it through tours and room-based interpretation centered on where Kate Morgan is believed to have stayed. A single room reference then linked the earlier guestbook artifact to physical space the public could repeatedly encounter.

The same cause-and-effect logic appears: documentation in, interpretation out. One entry in one ledger led to an official narrative package, then repeated guided experiences. That repetition did not erase uncertainty, but it made one room and one staircase into portable story locations for people who never saw the original hotel records.

From Administration Record to Threshold Object

In this single event, the guestbook became a threshold object because it operated at the border between rumor and procedure. It was not just a signed notebook; it was the first witness to the sequence and the first sign that an administrative object could carry symbolic weight after a tragedy.

What became the legend is a sequence of three actions: a recorded arrival, a tragic end with a missing check-out closure, and a public afterlife built through repeated tours and retellings. The motif persists because it is easy to picture and easy to check, even while some details remain disputed.

Aftereffect and Legacy of an Unfinished Stay

The chain did not produce certainty about motive or supernatural cause. It produced a durable pattern for legend: arrival logged, exit unresolved, place-memory institutionalized. As a result, guestbooks and similar records are now often treated as ritual thresholds in modern narrative studies, where the object matters less for one ghost claim and more for how it structures public belief over time.

This record therefore ends where archive work begins: at the boundary between what is verifiable, what is repeatable, and what a community keeps repeating.
