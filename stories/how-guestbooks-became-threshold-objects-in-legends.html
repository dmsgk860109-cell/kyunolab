The source chain is narrow and chronological: a front-desk event at the Hotel del Coronado, a delayed exit signal in public memory, then a long interpretive afterlife.

This sequence shows how a documented hospitality object can shift from ledger utility to legend threshold.

Front Desk Entry as a Threshold Record

In late 1892, the Hotel del Coronado received a guest who checked in under an assumed name. In hospitality practice, that line marks transition into the hotel's internal social space.

That one documented entry is the cause-stage of this story. It defines where private life becomes public infrastructure and where later retellings can begin to reuse an identifiable sequence.

Assumed Identity and Uncertain Initial Intent

The guest repeatedly returned to the desk context while waiting and describing an expected arrival, creating action points that are verifiable in the narrative chain.

Later interpretations differ on motive, but the record structure stays fixed: an arrival, multiple desk interactions, and a room stay that ended without a fully ordinary administrative closure.

The Stopping Point That Shaped the Story

Within five days, she was found dead on an exterior staircase leading toward the beach. The visible ending and the incomplete exit signal gave the event a durable narrative shape.

This is the strongest transition in the incident: the story moves from routine operations into a public, unresolved memory. The transition matters because legend formation often begins where procedure and ambiguity overlap.

Coroner Review and Identification Chain

Coroner and press-era accounts record a likely self-inflicted wound finding, while public reporting at the time included identification uncertainty and later confirmation.

The distinction is important. The medical and press sequence provides context, not supernatural finality. The chain remains: desk entry, disappearance of normal departure, and delayed identity clarification.

Tour Routines and the Memory Node

Over time, the hotel's guided material tied the event to specific rooms and an ongoing visitor experience path. The event moved from ledger record to interpretive narrative.

That aftereffect did not create the original chain, but it fixed the chain into repeatable memory by attaching it to physical room access, scheduled events, and public narration.

From Admin Record to Threshold Object

In modern folklore reading, this becomes a threshold-object case. The guestbook is less the supernatural trigger and more the durable object where entry, expectation, and uncertainty meet.

The strongest lesson is practical: one incomplete administrative sequence can become symbolic when communities keep re-reading the same threshold line through tours and witness routines.

Aftereffect and Legacy

The aftereffect does not erase investigative ambiguity. It makes the unresolved chain easier to remember, because the guestbook becomes the boundary object that appears in every retelling.

That is why this remains a Legend Origins record rather than a solved-mystery report: the chain is true as sequence, while interpretive layers remain contested but culturally active.

Q&A

What is the core evidence focus of this record?

A documented guest arrival at the Hotel del Coronado and the later sequence around identification and aftermath.

Where is the strongest factual boundary in the story?

The transition from arrival registration to public aftermath after a staircase death with delayed identity closure in common retellings.

Did this story become legend because of paranormal proof?

No. It became durable because one documented administrative trace was repeatedly mapped into place memory.

Why does the guestbook remain central?

It is the earliest shared artifact in a fixed sequence and a practical boundary marker of who entered the thresholded setting.

What is the takeaway for archive reading?

Keep cause, action, and aftermath separate from later ritual and tourism layers while preserving the single-event spine.
